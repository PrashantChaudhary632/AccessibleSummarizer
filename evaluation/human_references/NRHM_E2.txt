The National Rural Health Mission (NRHM) was launched to improve healthcare delivery in rural India, especially in states with weak health indicators and infrastructure. The framework focuses on strengthening public health systems by expanding access to essential services and improving quality of care. Key components include deploying Accredited Social Health Activists (ASHAs) in villages, strengthening Sub-Centres, Primary Health Centres (PHCs), and Community Health Centres (CHCs), and ensuring service guarantees under Indian Public Health Standards (IPHS). The mission emphasizes decentralised planning through Village Health Committees and District Health Action Plans, along with community participation to increase accountability. It also provides financial support through untied grants to local health institutions. The framework identifies issues such as low public spending and unregulated private healthcare, and aims to make healthcare more affordable and accessible for rural populations. Overall, NRHM seeks to achieve Health for All through systemic reforms and community engagement.
