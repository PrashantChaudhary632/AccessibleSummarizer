The Code of Criminal Procedure (CrPC), 1973 provides the procedural framework for the administration of criminal justice in India. It outlines the powers and responsibilities of courts, police officers, and other authorities in handling criminal cases. The Code specifies different types of courts and the powers they possess, including sentencing powers of judges and magistrates. It lays down detailed procedures for arrest, including when police can arrest without a warrant, rights of arrested persons such as being informed of grounds of arrest and right to bail, and safeguards like medical examination and production before a magistrate within 24 hours. It also includes provisions for public assistance to police, reporting of offences, and processes like summons and warrants to ensure the appearance of accused persons. Overall, the CrPC ensures a structured and fair process for investigation, trial, and punishment, while also protecting individual rights against misuse of power.
